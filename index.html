The problem

A few seconds of audio is now enough

Cloning a voice no longer needs a studio or a data scientist. It needs a few seconds of speech — a voicemail, a reel, a recorded meeting — and the result is convincing enough to carry an instruction that a human will act on.

01 A few seconds of voice Public audio of the person being impersonated.
02 AI voice clone Synthesised speech in that voice, on demand.
03 Trusted identity The caller now sounds like someone the target already trusts.
04 Fraud under time pressure A transfer, an OTP, an access grant — requested urgently.

Executive impersonation

A voice with authority asks finance to move money outside the normal process, today.

Customer impersonation

A cloned customer voice passes a call-centre check that was designed for humans.

Family-member scam

A familiar voice in distress asks for money immediately, and doubt feels cruel.

Official impersonation

A caller claiming police or government authority manufactures urgency and compliance.

Social engineering

The voice is only the opener; the attack is the sequence of requests that follows.

Why caller ID does not help

Number spoofing and a familiar voice defeat both halves of the check most people actually run — who the screen says is calling, and whether they sound right.

Why detection alone is not enough

Detectors get worse outside the lab

Published research shows the same detector performing far better on clean test sets than on real audio. Add a phone line, background noise and a short call, and the gap grows again. That gap is what VoxShield is designed around.

01 Clean test audio Where published results come from.
02 Real recordings Noise, distance, interruptions, short answers.
03 A phone line Compression strips detail the models rely on.
04 A number you cannot trust Still printed as a confident answer.

Not claimed

A single accuracy figure hides the conditions it was measured under, so VoxShield does not advertise one. Test results, their datasets and their limits are written up separately, for people who want to check the work.

How it stays silent

Section 1 of 2 · The claim

What VoxShield claims

One claim, in plain words — and the list of things we deliberately do not say.

What we do claim

Claimed

Four things, all of them checkable:

  • A check runs on call audio and produces a verdict about once a second.
  • The system refuses to answer when the audio cannot support an answer, and says why.
  • A refusal is not a pass — at high stakes it holds or blocks, like a detection would.
  • Nothing is uploaded: audio is analysed and dropped, and the decision is written to a tamper-evident log.

What we do not claim

Not claimed

Said plainly, so nobody has to guess:

  • No accuracy figure. We do not advertise a percentage.
  • No language coverage. We do not claim detection across languages.
  • No replay-attack detection.
  • No live carrier or bank integration — nothing here has ever stopped a real payment.
  • No certification, and no “blockchain”. The audit log is a hash chain.

Full technology breakdown

Precondition gate

Is this audio worth trusting at all?

The gate asks whether the window can support a conclusion — before any model score is read. Four checks, and a failure is never resolved in the reassuring direction.

Speech present≥ 2.5 s voiced in the 4.0 s window
Channel qualitySNR ≥ 10 dB
Clipping≤ 2% of samples
EnrolmentA voiceprint exists to compare against
Pass Analysis continues Signals are scored, fused and turned into a risk number with a verdict.
Fail LOW_CONFIDENCE A numeric range and one of six reason codes — INSUFFICIENT_SPEECH, CHANNEL_DEGRADED, NO_ENROLMENT, ANALYSIS_TIMEOUT, SIGNAL_DISAGREEMENT, NO_VALIDATED_SIGNAL.

Verdict system

Five answers, and one of them is “I cannot tell”

LOW_CONFIDENCE is not a softer SAFE. It carries no risk number, sets is_fake to null, and at high-stakes profiles it stops the transaction exactly as a detection would.

SAFE 0–39 is_fake boolean · nothing in the window argued for a clone
SUSPICIOUS 40–69 is_fake boolean · enough signal to change how the call is handled
HIGH_RISK 70–100 is_fake boolean · treat as an impersonation attempt
LOW_CONFIDENCE null + range is_fake: null · a reason code, never a score · not SAFE
ERROR null is_fake: null · the system failed, and says so
!

Three-valued by design. No failure path produces SAFE — not too little speech, not a degraded channel, not a model throw, a GPU out-of-memory, a queue overflow or signals that disagree by more than 45 points. A missing model file stops the process from starting at all.

Risk profile & mitigation

The same verdict, four different stakes

Detection produces a verdict. The risk profile decides what that verdict costs. This table is the product in one screen.

Prescribed action by risk profile and verdict
Risk profile SUSPICIOUS HIGH_RISK LOW_CONFIDENCE
Routine enquiry Log Flag to agent Log
Account change Flag to agent Hold + callback Prompt secondary question
High-value transfer Hold + callback Block + escalate Hold + callback
Privileged access Block + escalate Block + escalate Block + escalate

At the two strictest profiles, LOW_CONFIDENCE and SUSPICIOUS produce the identical action — which is the entire argument for making the refusal a first-class verdict.

Prescribed action

Implemented

The pipeline returns the verdict and the action the active profile prescribes, and the Console displays both. Changing the profile on a live call changes the prescribed action end to end.

Automated mitigation

Specified

Step-up verification, incidents, alerts and the mitigation engine are written up in the enterprise specification and are not built yet. When they land, every mitigation carries an integration_mode of simulated, pending_approval or integrated, and the UI prints it. No demo of this ever claims a real bank transaction was blocked.

VoxShield SDK

Planned

Integrate voice security anywhere. The intended path for banks, telecom operators and contact centres. It does not exist yet — no package, no key, no endpoint. The REST and WebSocket contract the Console already serves is what it would be built on.

What is decided so far

VoxShield Assistant

Built

Understand risk. Investigate faster. An offline, grounded assistant inside the Console. It returns a verbatim passage from the project's own documents with a citation, or it refuses with NO_GROUNDED_ANSWER. It never generates prose — the help system refuses to guess, exactly like the detector.

Measurements & limits What we do not claim

Security & privacy

Offline is enforced, not asserted

One outbound call exists in the entire codebase, in the model download script, at install time. Pull the network during the demo and nothing changes.

Memory only

Audio is analysed in memory and the window is discarded. No transcription, so no new class of personal data.

AES-256-GCM

Voiceprints are 192 floats, encrypted at rest, never returned by any API, and erasable cryptographically.

Hash-chained audit

SHA-256 chain; verification names the first broken link. Tamper-evident — and not called a blockchain.

15 / 15 hashes

Every model file is SHA-256 verified at load. A missing or altered model refuses to start the process.
