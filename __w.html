App detail · Voxshield Mobile

How a call is checked

Voxshield Mobile works with the audio of an active phone call and looks for signs that the voice on the other end is synthetic or cloned — running entirely on the device, in real time.

!

Product-level overview. The stages below describe what the app does at a product level. Internal model architecture, detection thresholds, and accuracy figures are not restated here — the VoxshieldMobile docs [PLACEHOLDER] stay the source of truth.

Stage by stage

What happens, in order

The same flow as the diagram, spelled out.

  1. 1

    Call audio

    During an active phone call, Voxshield Mobile works with the call audio as it arrives — there is no recording step and nothing to upload afterwards.

  2. 2

    Preprocessing

    Incoming audio is prepared for analysis as the call continues, so the check keeps pace with the conversation rather than lagging behind it.

  3. 3

    Voice analysis

    The app looks for signs that the speech is synthetic or cloned rather than naturally produced by a person speaking live.

  4. 4

    On-device verdict

    A result is produced on the phone itself. This is the core constraint of the design: the detection runs locally, not by shipping your call to a server.

  5. 5

    User alert

    If the call looks like a suspected impersonation attempt, the app surfaces a warning so you can act on it while the call is still happening.

  6. 6

    Enterprise SDK Planned

    The same detection capability, offered as an API/SDK for banks, telecom, and enterprise integration. This is on the roadmap and is not built — there is no download, key, or sign-up today.

Steps shown are a product-level description, not a specification of the internal implementation. Model architecture, feature design, and measured accuracy belong to the VoxshieldMobile [PLACEHOLDER] repository.

Design constraint

What stays on your device

The on-device requirement isn't a detail — it's the reason the approach is viable for calls at all.

On-device by design

Built

Detection runs in the Android app on the phone. Call audio is analyzed locally rather than sent to a remote service for scoring.
